The use case

A developer needs 100 MW near Houston.

Cheap land is easy to find. Land the grid can power, that won't flood, that neighbors won't fight, and that runs profitably: that's the hard part. Most teams answer it with three separate studies. SiteStar answers it as one decision.

Where can we build?

Hard gates on real land: flood zones, nearby homes, distance to the high-voltage grid. Fail one and the site is out.

02When power arrives · How it runs

Turn “wait for the grid” into “connect now.”

Say the utility can promise only 70 of your 100 MW during the grid's tightest hours. Normally the project waits for upgrades. If some of the computing can wait a few hours, the site can connect anyway, and running that work in cheaper hours cuts the power bill. Here is a real Houston day from ERCOT, and the schedule SiteStar's optimizer picks for it.

04Evidence

Every number shows its work.

Click any underlined number on this page to see where it came from: the source, when it was retrieved and how it was calculated. Each figure carries one of five labels.

Measured

Read from our own hardware: machine watts, job progress, drone camera.

Sourced

From a named dataset with a timestamp: ERCOT prices, FEMA flood zones.

Estimated

Derived by a documented method: distances, costs, fossil share.

Simulated

Scenario or replay: the 100 MW facility, grid limits, the miniature grid.

Utility-confirmed

From a utility interconnection study. None yet, and we say so.
